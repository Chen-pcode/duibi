An Ultra-Lightweight Cross-scale Attention

Mamba Network for Accurate Skin Lesion

Segmentation

Razan Alharith, Jiashu Zhang and Chengqiang Zhao

Abstract— Accurate skin lesion segmentation is essen-
tial for the early detection and effective management of
skin cancer. Existing deep learning architectures are con-
strained by a persistent computational–precision trade-off:
models either require heavy computation that limits prac-
tical deployment, or accept reduced accuracy when run-
ning in resource-limited environments. Recent approaches
reach strong performance by integrating advanced mod-
ules, but this also introduces complexity that restricts
practical use. We address this limitation with the Cross-
scale Attention Mamba Network (UCA-MNet), a hierarchi-
cal encoder–decoder that integrates three components:
a multi-scale feature encoder, a precision-focused fusion
decoder, and a Feature Compression and Fusion Mod-
ule (FCFM). The central component is the Multi-Scale
Module (MSM), which uses a bidirectional Mamba (Bi-
Mamba) to model long-range spatial dependencies with
linear computational complexity, a clear advantage over
the quadratic cost of transformer methods. Combined with
Cross-Scale Attention (CSA) and Pyramidal Squeeze At-
tention (PSA), UCA-MNet captures both fine-grained local
textures and global contextual information. Experiments
on the ISIC-2017, ISIC-2018, and PH2 benchmarks show
that UCA-MNet reaches F1 scores of 0.8254, 0.8814, and
0.9202, and mIoU scores of 0.8180, 0.8515, and 0.8604
respectively, using only 0.33 million parameters and 4.3
GFLOPs. This makes UCA-MNet about 83× smaller than
VM-UNET, a leading existing model, while achieving com-
petitive accuracy. The findings indicate that effective med-
ical image segmentation can be achieved with much lower
computational requirements, supporting deployment in
resource-constrained environments. The code is available
at: https://github.com/razanharith/UCA-MNet

Index Terms— Lightweight model, Skin lesion segmenta-
tion, Mamba, State Space Models (SSM), Attention Mecha-
nism, Feature Fusion, Hybrid Architecture.

The rising incidence, largely attributed to increased ultraviolet

skin lesion diagnosis relies heavily on the visual and dermo-

is subjective, experience-dependent, and time-consuming. The
gold standard diagnosis via biopsy and histopathology, while
accurate, is invasive, costly, and can delay timely treatment.
These challenges are particularly pronounced in resource-
limited settings and point to the need for objective, automated,
scalable diagnostic tools that can expand access to quality skin

In response, deep learning, especially Convolutional Neural

human experts [6], [7]. Despite this progress, widespread
clinical adoption remains held back by the heavy computa-
tional resources these models require. Typically, state-of-the-
art architectures contain millions of parameters and demand
high processing power, which limits their feasibility in real-
world clinical environments that often experience resource
limitations due to operational constraints [8]. These settings,
which include primary care offices, rural clinics, and health
centers in low and middle income countries (LMICs), do not
necessarily have access to high performance GPU servers.
They can also encompass point-of-care (POC) and mobile
health (mHealth) applications where the privacy of data and
network instability limit the potential to use cloud-based
inference [9], [10]. This implies that the models must execute
on non-specialized local hardware. This calls for a computa-
tionally efficient architecture with few parameters (to minimize
memory use) and few FLOPs (for fast inference), where low
latency and accessibility are critical. As one illustration, entry-
level smartphones commonly used in telemedicine applications
in LMICs typically allocate only 100–200MB for individual
application processes, so the difference between a 1.3MB
model (0.33M parameters) and a 5.5MB model (1.38M param-
eters) is decisive for deployment alongside imaging libraries
and user interface components. The key challenge, then, is to
develop models that maintain high accuracy while remaining
small enough and fast enough for large-scale deployment.

To address these challenges, we propose the Cross-scale
Attention Mamba Network (UCA-MNet), a lightweight
framework tailored for skin lesion segmentation. UCA-MNet
combines the local feature extraction strengths of CNNs
with the efficient global context modeling of a Mamba-
based State Space Model (SSM). It introduces a Multi-Scale
Module (MSM) built around a bidirectional Mamba (Bi-
Mamba), which captures long-range dependencies with linear
computational complexity. This avoids the quadratic cost of
conventional Transformers and lets UCA-MNet deliver wide
contextual understanding within an architecture small enough
for clinical deployment. The proposed UCA-MNet follows a
U-shaped encoder–decoder design with modules that target
both accuracy and efficiency: a hierarchical feature encoder, a
multi-scale fusion decoder built on the MSM, and a Feature
Compression and Fusion Module (FCFM), which together
extract and integrate rich multi-scale contextual information.
This design advances precise medical image segmentation
and provides a practical framework for automated diagnostic
systems that can support clinicians in timely decision-making.
Our key contributions are as follows:

1) A Hierarchical Encoder is proposed for the efficient ex-

and key projections alongside an expansion convolution,

(SE) blocks. Spatial details are preserved through skip
connections, enabling the effective capture of complex
patterns.
2) A Multi-Scale Module (MSM), forming a central com-

tegrating a bidirectional Mamba (Bi-Mamba). The mod-
ule captures long-range spatial dependencies with linear
complexity and is more efficient than transformer-based
methods. Cross-Scale Attention (CSA) and Pyramidal
Squeeze Attention (PSA) capture fine-grained, context-
aware features across multiple scales, which improves
segmentation performance.
3) A Multi-Scale Fusion Decoder is designed to reconstruct

segmentation maps by combining abstract encoder fea-
tures with progressively restored spatial details. Each
Decoder Block (D-Block) fuses high-resolution features
carried by skip connections with upsampled features

Module (FCFM) aggregates outputs from all decoder
blocks and refines the concatenated multi-scale features
through convolution operations, producing a coherent
feature map for the final segmentation.
4) UCA-MNet is validated through extensive comparisons
with state-of-the-art methods. Competitive mean Inter-
section over Union (mIoU) and F1 scores are achieved
across multiple benchmark datasets. This performance
is attained with only 0.33 million parameters and 4.3
GFLOPs, showing the model’s computational efficiency
and its potential for deployment in resource-constrained
environments.

II. RELATED WORK
This section reviews foundational and contemporary ap-
proaches in skin lesion segmentation. We first discuss the evo-
lution of deep learning models in this domain, then examine
the need for lightweight architectures and existing strategies.
We then turn to recent shifts toward models for long-range
dependency modeling, followed by a review of attention and
fusion mechanisms important for precise segmentation.

A. Deep Learning for Skin Lesion Segmentation

The automated segmentation of skin lesions has progressed
from traditional image processing techniques to deep learning-
based methods [14]. Early approaches based on thresholding,
region-growing, or edge detection often struggled with the
significant visual variance of lesions [15]. The advent of deep
learning, particularly Fully Convolutional Networks (FCNs)
[16], marked a significant turning point in the field.
The U-Net architecture [17], with its iconic encoder-decoder
structure and skip connections, became the de facto standard,
enabling precise localization by fusing semantic information
with high-resolution spatial details. Numerous variants have
since been proposed. U-Net++ [18] introduced nested and
dense skip connections to improve feature fusion, while others
have integrated components from ResNet and DenseNet to
strengthen feature extraction [19]. More recently, Transformer-
based models like FAT-Net [20] and SkinFormer [21] have
used self-attention mechanisms to better capture global con-
text. Despite their high accuracy, these advanced models are
often computationally intensive, which limits their practical
deployment. Purely convolutional models also continue to
struggle with the long-range dependencies needed to interpret
global lesion features such as asymmetry.

B. Lightweight Architectures for Medical Imaging

The successful deployment of deep learning models in clini-
cal settings, especially on mobile or edge devices, is contingent
on their computational efficiency. This has driven research
into lightweight architectures designed to minimize model
size, memory footprint, and inference time, often measured by
trainable parameters and Floating Point Operations (FLOPs).
A common strategy for creating lightweight models is the
use of depth-wise separable convolutions, famously employed
by MobileNet [8]. Other approaches, such as those seen in
MALU-Net [22] and ULFAC [23], have adapted these prin-
ciples for medical imaging. While these methods successfully
reduce computational load, they often do so by simplifying the
network’s architecture. This can lead to a fundamental trade-
off: the gain in efficiency may come at the cost of reduced
representational power, potentially compromising the model’s
ability to learn complex features and achieve the high accuracy
required for medical diagnosis.

C. Advancements in Long-Range Dependency Modeling

A primary limitation of traditional CNNs is their restricted
receptive field, making it difficult to model long-range spatial
relationships. Vision Transformers (ViTs) [24] addressed this
by using a self-attention mechanism to capture global context,
leading to significant performance gains. The self-attention
mechanism, however, has a computational complexity of
O(N 2) in the input sequence length, making Transformers
expensive and slow for high-resolution medical images.
A recent development that addresses this limitation is the
emergence of State Space Models (SSMs), particularly Mamba
[25]. Mamba is a new class of sequence model that captures
long-range dependencies with O(N) complexity. This opens
an opportunity to build architectures with the global con-
text modeling of Transformers inside a much more efficient,
lightweight framework. Our work, UCA-MNet, is designed

to take advantage of this development, building a dedicated
segmentation architecture around the Mamba framework.

D. Attention and Multi-Scale Fusion for Precise
Segmentation

Precise lesion segmentation, particularly at the boundaries,
requires a model that processes and integrates features from
multiple scales. The U-Net’s skip connections provided the
foundational blueprint for this multi-scale fusion. Attention
mechanisms then extended this by letting the model dynami-
cally focus on the most salient features.
Channel
attention
strategies,
such

the

Excitation (SE) block [26], recalibrate feature maps to em-
phasize important channels. More complex spatial attention
mechanisms help the model focus on critical regions of
an image. Combining such mechanisms effectively with a
long-range backbone like Mamba presents a new challenge.
UCA-MNet addresses this with a Multi-Scale Module (MSM)
that combines Cross-Scale Attention (CSA) and Pyramidal
Squeeze Attention (PSA) directly with a Bi-Mamba block.
This integration lets the model focus on important features
across different scales while also considering the lesion’s
global structure, producing precise boundary delineation with-
out a separate, post-hoc edge detection module.

A. Image Preprocessing Stage

All input images and ground truth (GT) masks are processed
through a unified preprocessing pipeline before training, vali-
dation, and testing. Portrait-oriented images (aspect ratio > 1)
are rotated by 90 degrees to landscape orientation to enforce
consistent spatial geometry across all samples. All images and
masks are then resized to (320 × 320) pixels.
During training, we apply a set of augmentation transforms to
improve generalization: random color inversion (applied with
20% probability), color jittering across brightness, contrast,
and hue, and random horizontal and vertical flipping (each
with 50% probability). GT masks are originally provided as
continuous values in [0,1] and are binarized using a fixed
threshold of 0.8, assigning lesion pixels a value of 1 and
background pixels a value of (0.8) To validate this threshold
choice, we conducted a sensitivity analysis over 50 randomly
sampled images per dataset, measuring the resulting change
in lesion area. Mean lesion area deviations remained below
2% across all datasets (ISIC-2017: 1.4%, ISIC-2018: 1.7%,
PH2: 1.2%), confirming that the threshold preserves boundary
integrity. Preprocessed data are batched and shuffled via Py-
Torch’s DataLoader throughout training to reduce overfitting
and promote generalization.

B. Network Design

To address the challenges of precise medical image segmen-
tation, we introduce the Cross-scale Attention Mamba Net-
work (UCA-MNet). Built on a hierarchical encoder–decoder

backbone, UCA-MNet’s central design choice is the tight cou-
pling of a Mamba-based attention module that captures both
fine-grained local texture details and wide global contextual
information. As illustrated in Fig.(2-A), the network architec-
ture is composed of three primary components: a hierarchical
feature encoder, a multi-scale fusion decoder, and a feature ag-
gregation module. At a high level, features propagate through
the network as follows. The input image X ∈R3×H×W first

that halves the spatial dimensions and projects the input to

1) Hierarchical Encoder for Multi-Scale Feature Extraction:
The encoder’s principal function within UCA-MNet is to ex-
tract hierarchical features from the input image across multiple
scales. After the initial convolution that produces F0, the
network passes the data through a sequence of four Encoder
Blocks (E-Blocks).

Each E-Block, shown in Fig.(2-B), processes its input
feature map Fi−1 along two parallel computational paths. The
primary path applies a 1×1 convolution to reduce the channel
dimension to C/4, then generates query (Q) and key (K)
projections. When the channel dimension exceeds a predefined
threshold (controlled by the if True condition in Fig. 2-B),
these projections activate the Multi-Scale Module (MSM),
which performs context-aware feature extraction incorporating
long-range dependencies. When the condition is not met, a
1 × 1 convolution is applied instead, keeping the module
lightweight at early stages. In parallel, the secondary path
applies an expansion convolution (kernel size 3, expanding
to 3 × C), which increases channel capacity and improves
the model’s ability to represent complex features. The outputs
from both paths are concatenated along the channel dimen-
sion and refined using a Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) block,
which recalibrates channel importance through global average
pooling followed by two fully connected layers. The refined
feature map Ei serves two purposes: it is forwarded to the
next E-Block for continued hierarchical encoding, and it is
simultaneously transmitted to the corresponding D-Block via
a skip connection, preserving fine-grained spatial details for
the decoder.

2) Multi-Scale Fusion Decoder: The decoder reconstructs
the segmentation map by combining abstract, high-level en-
coder features with progressively restored spatial details. This
process is achieved through a series of four Decoder Blocks
(D-Blocks), each integrated with the Multi-Scale Module
(MSM).

a) D-Block Structure: As depicted in Fig.(2-C), each D-
Block at level i receives two inputs: (1) the skip-connected
features Ei from the corresponding E-Block, and (2) the
upsampled features ˆDi+1 from the preceding (deeper) D-
Block. For the deepest D-Block (i = 4), the second input is
the final encoder output E4 itself. The skip-connected features
Ei are first refined by two parallel dilated convolutions with
dilation rates d1 and d2, which expand the effective receptive
field before fusion. These enhanced features are concatenated
and combined with ˆDi+1 through element-wise addition. The
fused representation then passes through the MSM via query
and key projections, followed by a 1×1 convolution, producing
the decoder output Di. This fusion strategy ensures that rich
semantic context from deeper layers is integrated with fine-
grained spatial information from the encoder at every decoding
step.

b) Multi-Scale Module (MSM): The Multi-Scale Module
(MSM) serves as the core computational unit shared by both
the encoder and decoder. It is activated when the input feature
map’s channel dimension exceeds the threshold, ensuring com-
putational resources are allocated where multi-scale reasoning
is most beneficial. Internally, as shown in the MSM detail of
Fig. 2, the module processes features through the following

pipeline. Given query (Q) and key (K) projections from
the parent block, a value representation V is first computed
as V = −PReLU(−PReLU(Q + K)), which introduces
non-linear feature interaction between the two projections.
This value map V is then processed sequentially through
four sub-components: (1) Cross-Scale Attention (CSA), which
captures inter-scale feature relationships by computing atten-
tion weights across features at different spatial scales; (2)
Pyramidal Squeeze Attention (PSA), which extracts multi-scale
spatial information through parallel pooling at multiple kernel
sizes, enabling the model to attend to features at varying
granularities; (3) Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE), which recali-
brates channel-wise responses; and (4) Bidirectional Mamba
(Bi-Mamba), which models long-range spatial dependencies
by scanning the feature sequence in both forward and back-
ward directions with linear computational complexity O(N),
avoiding the quadratic cost O(N 2) of transformer-based self-
attention. The output is stabilized by Group Normalization
(GN) followed by PReLU activation. This sequential design
allows the MSM to capture fine-grained local textures (via
CSA and PSA), channel-wise saliency (via SE), and global
spatial context (via Bi-Mamba) within a single, lightweight
module.

3) Feature Compression and Fusion Module (FCFM): The
final component of the UCA-MNet architecture is the Feature
Compression and Fusion Module (FCFM), which aggregates
information across all decoder scales into a unified segmenta-
tion prediction. The four D-Block outputs {D1, D2, D3, D4}
represent features at different spatial resolutions. The FCFM
first upsamples all outputs to the same spatial resolution
(matching D1) and concatenates them along the channel
dimension to form a full multi-scale representation Dcat ∈
R(C1+C2+C3+C4)× H

4 × W
4 . A series of 1 × 1 convolutions then
compresses and fuses these features into a single coherent
feature map. This fused output is projected to a single channel
and upsampled to the original input resolution, producing
the final segmentation map Y ∈R1×H×W . By aggregating
features from all decoder levels, the FCFM ensures that
the final prediction benefits from both high-level semantic
understanding (from deeper D-Blocks) and fine spatial detail
(from shallower D-Blocks).

This section provides a full overview of the implemen-
tation, datasets, comparative models, evaluation measures,
experimental results, ablation studies, and model complexity,
supported by multiple figures. Results for the comparative

A. Implementation Details

a) Datasets: To evaluate the proposed model, we used
three publicly available and widely adopted benchmarks for
skin lesion segmentation: ISIC-2017 [27], a public dataset
of 2,000 dermoscopic images; ISIC-2018 [28], with 3,694
images; and PH2 [29], with 202 dermoscopic images. These
three datasets were selected for the following reasons. First,
they are the most widely used benchmarks in this field; a
survey of 177 skin lesion segmentation studies [30] reports
that ISIC-2017 appears in 39.4% of papers, ISIC-2018 in
21.2%, and PH2 in 18.9%, which makes them the dominant
evaluation benchmarks and ensures direct comparability with
published results. Second, they provide scale diversity: dataset
sizes range from 202 (PH2) to 3,694 (ISIC-2018) images,
enabling evaluation under both data-limited and data-sufficient
conditions, which is important for assessing a lightweight
model’s generalization ability. Third, ISIC-2017 and ISIC-
2018 provide official pre-defined train/validation/test parti-
tions, which ensures reproducibility and eliminates variabil-
ity due to random splitting. Fourth, these datasets exhibit
clinical diversity, containing images acquired with different
dermoscopic systems and covering a range of lesion types
(melanoma, benign nevi, seborrheic keratoses) with varying
levels of artifacts such as hair occlusion and low contrast,
which tests the model’s robustness under realistic conditions.
For our experiments, all images were resized to a standard
resolution of (320 × 320) pixels, and the datasets have been
used with the following pre-existing train/validation/test splits
as provided in Table I.

all models against three primary criteria: model size (number

• H2Former [31] introduces a hierarchical hybrid trans-
former architecture tuned for medical segmentation. It
combines convolutional layers with multi-scale self-
attention to capture both local and global context. With
about 33.7 million parameters and 33.56 GFLOPs, it is
well-suited to high-resolution segmentation tasks while
keeping reasonable computational cost.

• VM-UNET [32] (Vision Mamba UNet) is a U-shaped
architecture that incorporates state space models, specifi-
cally the Visual State Space (VSS) block, to capture long-
range contextual information. It features an asymmetric
encoder-decoder design with patch embedding, merging,
and expanding operations, supported by simple skip
connections. This structure balances efficient dependency
modeling and linear computational complexity, often out-
performing traditional CNN and Transformer methods in
segmentation tasks. It has 27.43 million parameters.

• VM-UNET-V2 [33] extends the original VM-UNet by
improving the VSS block and introducing a Semantics
and Detail Infusion (SDI) mechanism. This allows tighter
fusion of low- and high-level features while keeping
the linear-complexity long-range modeling of the orig-
inal. Experimental results across several medical image
datasets show that VM-UNet-V2 reaches competitive
performance with stronger feature integration. It contains
23.16 million parameters.

• HED (Holistically-Nested Edge Detection) [34] is primar-
ily designed for edge detection. It uses deep supervision
and multi-scale feature fusion to produce precise bound-
ary localization. With over 14 million parameters, HED
can be adapted for tasks that require accurate edge-aware
segmentation.

• EMCADNet [35] is a lightweight yet effective decoder
tailored for medical image segmentation. It combines
multi-scale depth-wise convolutions with channel, spatial,
and large-kernel grouped gated attention mechanisms.

This design strengthens feature representation and high-
lights salient regions efficiently. When paired with a
standard encoder, it has 3.92 million parameters and 1.28
GFLOPs.

• CMUNeXt [36] is a compact convolutional neural net-
work that uses compound scaling and multi-scale fea-
ture aggregation to improve segmentation accuracy. It
combines efficient depthwise separable convolutions with
new normalization techniques. The result is a model with
3.15 million parameters and 11.28 GFLOPs, reducing
computational cost without sacrificing performance.

• Rolling Unet S [37] extends the classic U-Net by in-
troducing a rolling mechanism that iteratively refines
feature maps through residual connections. This strategy
promotes feature reuse and improves gradient flow, lead-
ing to better segmentation accuracy. The model balances
complexity and inference speed, containing 1.78 million
parameters and requiring 3.22 GFLOPs.

• UNeXt [38] presents a hybrid convolutional-MLP archi-
tecture for medical image segmentation. It begins with
convolutional layers, followed by tokenized MLP blocks
that efficiently process and project features while cap-
turing local dependencies through channel shifting. The
model uses 1.47 million parameters and 0.57 GFLOPs,
preserving skip connections to maintain spatial resolution.

• ShuffleNet-v2 [39] is an efficient model developed
through systematic controlled experiments focused on
performance across desktop and mobile platforms. This
compact architecture contains only 1.38 million trainable
parameters.

• LightMUNet [40] is a lightweight fusion model that
combines the UNet backbone with features of the Mamba
model, aiming for efficient medical image segmentation.
It reduces the parameter count to 1.15 million, enabling
faster inference while maintaining accuracy on both 2D
and 3D datasets. This model is designed to balance
complexity and segmentation effectiveness for practical
applications.

I. Ablation Studies

To evaluate the contribution of each architectural component
in UCA-MNet, we performed an ablation study on the ISIC-
2017 dataset. We constructed five model variants by selec-
tively incorporating or omitting key modules, as defined in
Table V. Baseline denotes a standard convolutional encoder-
decoder without any proposed modules; B+MSM adds only
the Multi-Scale Module (comprising Bi-Mamba, CSA, and
PSA) to the baseline; B+FEM adds the Feature Enhancement
Module, which implements the dual-path encoder with SE-
block refinement; B+EEA adds the Efficient Encoder Archi-
tecture, comprising the hierarchical E-Block structure without
the MSM; and B+ASPP replaces the MSM with an Atrous
Spatial Pyramid Pooling module as an alternative multi-scale
feature extractor. All variants share the same decoder and loss
function, differing only in the specified encoder component.
Each variant was trained and evaluated independently under
identical conditions, with performance assessed using Recall,
Precision, F1-score, and mean Intersection over Union (mIoU),
and efficiency measured by parameter count, FLOPs, and
inference time.
Table V summarises the results. The Baseline achieves a
mIoU of 0.5510 and a Recall of 0.6398 but performs well
below the full architecture. Adding individual modules pro-
duces mixed results that reveal the architectural dependencies
within UCA-MNet. B+MSM reduces Recall from 0.6398 to

0.4995 and F1-score from 0.5053 to 0.4877, while B+ASPP
produces a broader decline across metrics (F1: 0.4512 vs.
0.5053). These reductions reflect the tight coupling within
UCA-MNet’s design rather than any module failure: the MSM
was calibrated to receive multi-scale features from our dual-
path FEM encoder, so when attached to the generic baseline
encoder, the Bi-Mamba component receives feature distribu-
tions that violate its design assumptions, and the absent FCFM
pathway prevents the global context it produces from reaching
the decoder. The variants that show partial standalone gains
B+FEM (improved Precision and F1) and B+EEA (modest
mIoU gain) interact less tightly with these encoder assump-
tions, yet their improvements remain limited without the full
system. The strongest evidence for each component’s necessity
is the complete architecture itself: UCA-MNet achieves the
highest performance across all four metrics Recall (0.8063),
Precision (0.9204), F1-score (0.8254), and mIoU (0.8180)
while simultaneously requiring the fewest parameters (0.33 M,
a 73.8% reduction from the baseline’s 1.26 M) and lowest
FLOPs (4.3 G vs. 15.28 G) of any variant. Removing any com-
ponent lowers accuracy, yet the complete design costs fewer
resources than every partial version. The modules work as a
co-designed system, not as independently stackable blocks.

To verify that the reported results are not attributable to a
favourable random initialisation, we conducted three further
training runs using different random seeds (7, 42, 99), holding
all hyperparameters constant. Table VI reports test-set results
across all four runs, including the main run. The mean F1-
score is 81.28±1.49% and the mean mIoU is 80.52±1.27%,
both with standard deviations below 1.5%, confirming stable
convergence across initialisations. The higher variance in
Recall (±3.30%) reflects the known sensitivity of this metric
to the decision threshold and does not affect the stability of
F1 or mIoU, which are the primary evaluation metrics. The
main reported result (F1: 82.54%, mIoU: 81.80%) sits within
this range, so it is representative rather than a single lucky
run. Across all four runs, UCA-MNet consistently outperforms
the best-performing ablation variant (B+FEM, F1 = 51.22%)
by a minimum margin of +28.08% F1. The advantage of the
full architecture over every ablation variant is therefore stable
across random initialisations, not an artefact of seed choice.

higher than the baseline (0.0160 s) despite having substantially
lower FLOPs (4.3 G vs. 15.28 G). This apparent tension

V. DISCUSSION

UCA-MNet achieves competitive skin lesion segmentation
performance across standard benchmarks with only 0.33 mil-
lion trainable parameters and 4.3 GFLOPs. These results show
that high-quality segmentation is possible under strict resource
constraints, without relying on the large model capacities
typical of Mamba-based or transformer architectures. The
ablation experiments (Table V) confirm that this performance
comes from the integration of interdependent components
rather than from any single module in isolation, with the
complete system achieving a 63% F1 improvement and a
73.8% parameter reduction relative to the baseline. Among
Mamba-based models, UCA-MNet achieves strong segmen-
tation accuracy with far fewer parameters. VM-UNET and
VM-UNET-V2 require 27.43 M and 23.16 M parameters,
respectively, compared to just 0.33 M for UCA-MNet making
UCA-MNet approximately 83× smaller than VM-UNET. VM-
UNET scores slightly higher on ISIC-2017 (F1: 0.8500, mIoU:
0.8194 vs. 0.8254, 0.8180), but UCA-MNet outperforms it on

ISIC-2018 (F1: 0.8814, mIoU: 0.8515 vs. 0.8659, 0.8386).
UCA-MNet also exceeds LightMUNet (1.15 M parameters,
4.28 GFLOPs) on both ISIC-2017 and ISIC-2018, showing
that competitive accuracy is achievable with 3.5× fewer pa-
rameters.
This level of compression has concrete implications for real-
world clinical deployment. At float32 precision, UCA-MNet
occupies just 1.3 MB of memory, compared to 5.5 MB for
a model with 1.38 M parameters such as ShuffleNetV2. This
distinction matters when deploying on entry-level mobile de-
vices (smartphones with 2–4 GB RAM) or embedded systems
(e.g., Raspberry Pi 4 with 4 GB RAM), which are common in
primary care offices, rural clinics, and health centers in low-
and middle-income countries (LMICs). Such environments
typically allocate only 100–200 MB per application process
shared across imaging libraries, user interface components,
and patient data management so a 4.2 MB difference in
model size is significant. These results show that competitive
segmentation performance does not require large models or
heavy computation. UCA-MNet balances accuracy, model
size, and computational cost. Its low memory footprint, strong
benchmark performance, and minimal inference requirements
make it suitable for deployment in mobile health (mHealth)
platforms, point-of-care diagnostic devices, and resource-
constrained clinical systems where on-device inference is
necessary due to data privacy regulations and limited network
connectivity.

VI. LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK

UCA-MNet shows that competitive skin lesion segmentation
can be achieved with as few as 0.33M parameters an order of
magnitude fewer than the lightest models in our comparison
(1.15M to 27.43M). This extreme parameter efficiency is
the central contribution of this work, but it also defines
the scope of its current limitations. All experiments were
conducted on intra-dataset splits: the official ISIC partitions

segmentation improves diagnostic workflows in practice. This

model’s clinical applicability.

VII. CONCLUSION

We introduced UCA-MNet, a lightweight deep learning
architecture designed for efficient skin lesion segmentation
on benchmark datasets. The model is built as a hierarchical
encoder–decoder with a Mamba-based attention module that
integrates detailed local texture information with broad global
context. The main strengths of UCA-MNet come from three
design elements: a multi-scale hierarchical encoder that ex-
tracts features at several resolutions, a multi-scale fusion de-
coder built to reconstruct high-resolution segmentation maps,
and a Feature Compression and Fusion Module (FCFM) that
aggregates features across all decoder scales. Central to the
encoder is the Multi-Scale Module (MSM), which uses a bidi-
rectional Mamba (Bi-Mamba) attention mechanism together
with Cross-Scale Attention (CSA) and Pyramidal Squeeze
Attention (PSA). This combination captures long-range spatial
dependencies efficiently with linear computational complexity.
Skip connections preserve spatial fidelity by passing informa-
tion directly between the encoder and decoder blocks.

Evaluations on ISIC-2017, ISIC-2018, and PH2 show that
UCA-MNet reaches competitive performance compared with
existing lightweight segmentation models and accuracy com-
parable to much more complex architectures. Its compact
design, with only 0.33 million parameters and 4.3 GFLOPs,
allows efficient training and inference on resource-constrained
devices. The hybrid loss function that combines binary cross-
entropy (BCE), Dice, and intersection over union (IoU) losses
also improves the model’s sensitivity to complex and irregular
lesion boundaries. Future work will focus on cross-dataset
generalization experiments, systematic failure case analysis,
and prospective real-world clinical validation studies. With
its integrated and computationally efficient architecture, UCA-
MNet provides a foundation for the development of specialized
lightweight networks in medical image segmentation.
